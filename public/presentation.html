It already runs the group's money

Not a prototype. The members on the register are contributing against it, and every figure below is computed from records the app holds, not typed in by hand.

Joining takes a minute

A new member opens the app, types their first name, sees their own seat confirmed, and sets their phone number and a four-digit PIN. No admin has to register anyone.

  • Seat can only be claimed once, ever
  • Setting up replaces the placeholder login entirely
  • Claiming can never grant you a role

Contributions, chased automatically

Monthly dues are raised on their own once the year starts. Late ones pick up the fine the constitution specifies, and the treasurer can see the month without asking anyone.

  • Rates differ for secondary members, per constitution
  • Meeting fees are tracked separately
  • Financial year runs July to June

Money out needs two signatures

A withdrawal is typed in and stays pending until a second officer signs it. The person who recorded it cannot be the one who approves it.

  • Bank balance ignores a withdrawal until it's signed
  • Every signature is stamped with who and when

Leaving is a vote, not a favour

A member requests to exit from their profile. The app works out the refund from what they actually paid, the group votes as active members, and the payout closes the membership.

  • One member, one vote — never weighted by amount
  • Arrears deducted only if unwaived
  • You cannot vote on your own exit

Announcements and proposals

Leadership posts announcements; anyone can reply. Proposals for spending get threaded discussion before they reach a vote, and the forum links back to the withdrawal they became.

  • Announcements can be pinned
  • Officer titles shown beside the author

Meeting minutes do the work

Take minutes in the app. Once attendance is marked, the discipline officer can raise absence fines straight from the record instead of retyping the room.

  • Virtual and AGM meetings distinguished
  • Draft until the secretary marks it official

Where the group stands

The four numbers that matter

These are not stored totals. Each is derived from the underlying records by a published formula, so any member can check the arithmetic against the transactions behind it.

Cash on hand

= opening cash + bank withdrawals − cash expenses − bank deposits

Bank balance

= opening bank + contributions collected + bank deposits − M-Pesa expenses − bank withdrawals

Total group funds

= cash on hand + bank balance + investments value

AGM Party Fund

= opening balance + meeting-fee contributions − AGM Party expenses

A slice of the totals above, not an extra pot.

One number on screen is not quite the whole picture

Where the group has invested, the app pairs each investment with the bank movement that funded it. Until that pairing is automatic, investments can be counted twice in the headline total — a real overstatement, being fixed at source before it can mislead anyone.

Work completed

What we hardened before handing it over

Money apps fail quietly. The interesting work was not adding screens — it was finding the places where a member could change a number they shouldn't, and closing each one.

  • ✦ Your PIN never reaches your phone Sign-in is verified on the server against a one-way hash. No member can read, copy or crack another member's PIN, because no PIN is ever sent to a browser.
  • ✦ One member can no longer vote twice Voting used to rely on a list that treated two near-identical entries as two people. Voting now happens inside a single transaction, so the check that decides is the check that commits. This was the defect that could have carried an exit request on one member's vote alone.
  • ✦ The two-signature rule actually works It was inert. A withdrawal moved the bank balance the moment it was typed in, before anyone approved it. It now stays pending until a second officer signs — and the person who recorded it cannot sign their own.
  • ✦ Only an admin can close a membership Any signed-in member could previously mark anyone inactive, which quietly shrinks the pool every later exit vote is counted against. That decision is server-side now, and admin-only.
  • ✦ Your name on a post is the server's to state Forum replies and withdrawal records took their author from whatever the browser said. Identity is now read from your member record.
  • ✦ Changing your PIN ends your other sessions Sessions are revocable server-side. Change your PIN and every other device is logged out — the standard behaviour when a credential is replaced.
  • ✦ Nobody can sign in as you with the old default PIN Seats arrived carrying a placeholder phone and a default PIN, both guessable. Until a member claims their seat those were live credentials. Claiming replaces them and removes the old hash — which is why the admin screen now flags anyone still unclaimed.

Constraints, not preferences

The rules this app holds to

A co-operative is not a startup with a constitution attached. Some of these are settled law, and the app is built around them rather than against them.

Contributions, never shares

A contribution is a deposit — money the Act expects to be repaid. A share is permanent equity, capped at a fifth per member, requiring a register and attracting a dividend. They are different instruments under the Act, and this app pays refunds on exit, which is exactly what makes them deposits. Calling them shares would misdescribe them to members, to an auditor and to the tax office.

One member, one vote

Irrespective of what anyone has contributed. The exit threshold counts active members as people, never as shillings — and a member cannot vote on their own exit.

Nothing is deleted

The database refuses deletion outright. A mistake is corrected by adding a correcting entry, so the history a member can inspect stays whole.

Kenyan numbers only

+254 phone numbers normalised on the way in, shillings with no currency conversion anywhere, and a financial year that runs July to June.

Where we are not compliant yet

Fines are currently raised automatically when a contribution is late. Under the Data Protection Act that is an automated decision with legal effect, and members are entitled to written notice of it, an explanation in plain language, and a route to have a person reconsider it. The Co-operative Societies Act separately requires notice and an opportunity to show cause before a fine is imposed.

Today we do none of that, and it is live. The fix is written and it is small — a notice naming the rule and the amount, a plain-language explanation, and a named officer who answers challenges in writing. It sits in the current gate, not a later one, because it is the thing a regulator would look at first.
