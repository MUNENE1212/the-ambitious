## 1. The governing law

The governing statute is the **Co-operative Societies Act, Cap. 490**, assented 22 December 1997, commenced 1 June 1998 (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1997/12/eng@2023-12-11, retrieved 2026-10-20). Kenya Law marks this as "the latest version of this Act", currency date **11 December 2023**. Amendment history shown on that page:

- Co-operative Societies (Amendment) Act, 2004 (**Act No. 2 of 2004**) — note **No. 2**, not "No. 16"
- Sacco Societies Act (Cap. 490B) — 26 Sep 2009
- Companies and Insolvency Legislation (Consequential Amendments) Act (No. 19 of 2015) — 3 Jul 2017
- Finance Act 2017 (No. 15 of 2017) — 1 Jan 2018
- Revised by24th Annual Supplement (Legal Notice 221 of 2023) — 31 Dec 2022

s.96 repeals the Co-operative Societies Act, 1966, with savings for existing societies and by-laws.

**Answer: the 2024 Act is not in force because it does not exist. The 2004 Act is not "No. 16 of 2004" — it is No. 2 of 2004, and it is a 22-section amendment, not a replacement statute. Cap. 490 remains the operative law.**

## 2. Registrar / registration process — PARTIALLY UNVERIFIED

The registration functionary is the **Commissioner for Co-operative Development**, an office in the public service (s.3(1)), assisted by deputy Commissioners (s.3(2)) and answerable to the **Cabinet Secretary for Co-operative development** (s.2). Kenya Law records no separate "Registrar of Co-operatives" title.

Statutory requirements (all verified from Cap. 490):

- **s.5(a)** — a *primary society* must "consist of at least **ten persons** all of whom shall be qualified for membership … under section 14"
- **s.4** — the society's objects must promote member welfare/economic interests, and its **by-laws must incorporate all seven co-operative principles** (voluntary and open membership; democratic member control; economic participation; autonomy and independence; education/training/information; co-operation among co-operatives; concern for community)
- **s.6(1)** — application to the Commissioner in prescribed form, signed by ≥10 qualified persons
- **s.6(2)** — must be accompanied by **four copies of the proposed by-laws in English**
- **s.7** — the Commissioner may grant **provisional registration for up to one year**
- **s.11(1)** — the certificate of registration is **conclusive evidence** of registration; **s.11(2)** it must be **displayed at the head office**
- **s.12** — on registration the society becomes **a body corporate** with perpetual succession and a common seal

Categories are defined in s.2: **primary society** (membership restricted to individuals — this covers a ~13-member group), **co-operative union** (membership restricted to primary societies), **apex society** (national-level). s.4 proviso: a union or apex society shall not be registered except with limited liability.

## 3. Shares vs guarantee; contribution vs subscription — the key distinction

**Both are provided for.** s.2 defines *"limited liability"* as "limited **by shares** or limited **by guarantee**, according to the nature of the liability **prescribed by the by-laws**". The choice is made in the by-laws, not by the Registrar.

- **s.2 "share"** — "the amount represented by a member's **portion in the equity** of a society **as a co-owner**"
- **s.2 "capital"** — "the **permanent** members equity in the form of common stock and includes all disclosed reserves, retained earnings, grants or donations"
- **s.2 "dividend"** — a member's share of surplus "calculated by reference to the proportion which that member's **share capital** bears to the total share capital"
- **s.2 "bonus"** — a member's share of surplus "calculated by reference to the proportion which that member's **volume of business** with the society bears to the total"

So a **share is permanent equity** that ranks for dividends pro-rata to capital. **s.47(4)** reinforces this: the reserve fund "shall be **indivisible** and no member shall be entitled to claim a specific share of it."

On by-shares mechanics:
- **s.15** — no member (other than a co-operative society) may hold more than **one-fifth of the issued and paid-up share capital**
- **s.20** — transfer/charge of a member's share is subject to s.15 conditions; for an **unlimited liability** society, a member may not transfer until held ≥1 year and only in favour of the society or a member
- **s.39** — on death, the share/interest passes to a nominee or personal representative, or is paid out in the unlimited-liability case
- **s.91(2)(l)** — rules must provide for a register of members and, where liability is limited by shares or guarantee, **a register of shares**
- **s.40** — a register of members or shares is **prima facie evidence** of the date a person became/ceased to be a member and **the number of shares held**

### Contribution vs share subscription — the legally decisive answer

**They are not the same thing, and the Act's own definitions draw the line.**

1. **A contribution is not equity.** The Act contains **zero occurrences of "withdraw"**, **zero of "share certificate"**, and the only "saving" hits are Post Office Savings Bank and "repeal and savings". There is **no withdrawal mechanism for member savings anywhere in Cap. 490.** A monthly contribution the member expects to get back is a **recoverable debt/deposit**, not capital.
2. **s.2 "deposit"** — "a sum of money paid on terms under which it **shall be repaid**, with or without interest or premium, and either **on demand** or at a time or in circumstances agreed". This is the statutory category for repayable member money. A contribution repayable on demand fits this definition; a share does not.
3. **s.44** — a society may take loans from non-members only as prescribed, and **"a deposit of money under a hire-purchase agreement shall be deemed to be a loan."** This shows repayable member money is treated as an obligation, not equity.
4. **s.17** — a member may not exercise rights until they "have made such payment to the society in respect of membership, **or has acquired such interest in the society** as may be prescribed … or under the by-laws". Admission payment and capital subscription are separate limbs.
5. **s.91(2)(c)** — rules prescribe "the payments to be made and the **interest to be acquired** before the exercise of the right of membership"; **s.91(2)(d)** rules "regulate the manner in which funds may be raised **whether by means of shares or debentures or otherwise**". The Act expressly contemplates raising funds **otherwise than by shares**.
6. **s.32** — fines are a "**civil debt** due to the co-operative society", recoverable summarily, and may be set off "against any moneys due to such member in respect of produce delivered by him". Note the set-off is expressed against **produce**, not against share capital.
7. **s.46(2)** — no dividend, bonus or distribution of accumulated funds without a balance sheet and audited account. **s.46(3)** — a dividend is paid "at such rate as may be recommended by the management committee and **approved by the annual general meeting**".

**Practical consequence:** a recurring monthly contribution/savings deposit is legally a repayable liability of the society (s.2 "deposit"), not member capital. Calling it a "share" would misdescribe it, because shares are permanent equity (s.2 "capital"/"share"), carry a one-fifth holding cap (s.15), appear on a share register (s.91(2)(l)), and attract a capital-proportional dividend (s.2 "dividend"). Switching from "contributions" to "shares/subscriptions" is a substantive change in the group's legal structure, not a relabelling.

## 4. Minimum membership and capital

- **Minimum membership: ten persons.** s.5(a) (primary society); s.6(1)(a) (application signed by ≥10). **VERIFIED.** A ~13-member group clears this.
- **Minimum paid-up capital: the Act sets no figure.** s.5(b) requires a union to have ≥2 registered societies; **s.5(c) was deleted by Act No. 2 of 2004, s.4.** This is why the current s.5 reads only "(a) … [Act No. 2 of 2004, s. 4.]". **The statutory capital floor was removed in 2004.** Any current minimum-capital figure must therefore come from subsidiary rules or the Registrar's practice — **UNVERIFIED**.

### The critical procedural point for your by-law amendments

**An amendment is not valid until the Commissioner registers it.** s.8(2): "No amendment of the by-laws … shall be valid until the amendment has been **registered** under this Act, for which purpose a copy of the amendment shall be forwarded to the Commissioner". s.8(3) the Commissioner registers it if not contrary to the Act or rules. **s.8(3A)** allows the Commissioner to **cancel** an amendment effected by misrepresentation or concealment of a material fact. s.8(5) he issues a certified copy, "conclusive evidence".

So an **AGM/special-general-meeting vote alone is insufficient** — passing the resolution is step one, registration with the Commissioner is the step that makes it effective. Separately, s.2 defines **"special resolution"** as "a resolution passed by **two thirds of the members present and voting** at a general meeting", and s.27(4) requires ≥15 days' written notice to convene a general meeting. s.13: once registered, by-laws bind every member "as if they were signed by each member".

## Bottom line for the product decision

The governing law is **Cap. 490 (1997) as amended, principally by Act No. 2 of 2004** — there is no 2024 Act. Ten members is the statutory floor, cleared by 13. The by-laws, not the Registrar, decide shares-vs-guarantee and set the reserve percentage. **Monthly contributions are repayable deposits (s.2), not share subscriptions** — the Act has no withdrawal provision, and shares are permanent equity capped at one-fifth per member with capital-proportional dividends. Most importantly for this group: **by-law changes require Commissioner registration (s.8(2)), not merely an AGM vote**, and **you cannot audit accounts at the AGM without prior submission to the Commissioner (s.25(7))**.
