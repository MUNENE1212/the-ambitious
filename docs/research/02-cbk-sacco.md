# CBK / Kenya regulation of deposit-taking SACCO societies — verified research

**All retrievals dated 2026-10-20.**

## ⚠️ Threshold finding: the premise of the question is incorrect

**The Central Bank of Kenya does not license SACCO societies.** Deposit-taking SACCO societies are licensed and supervised by the **Sacco Societies Regulatory Authority (SSRA)**, an autonomous body corporate established by statute, not by CBK.

This is not an inference — it is directly evidenced three ways:

1. **CBK's own licensing index carries no SACCO category.** `https://www.centralbank.go.ke/regulations-and-licensing/` → "Licensing Procedures" and "Licensed Institutions" cover only: commercial banks, mortgage finance institutions, authorised non-operating bank holding companies, microfinance banks, credit reference bureaus, forex bureaus, money remittance providers, digital credit providers, representative offices. **No SACCO societies.** Likewise the CBK legislation index (`/policy-procedures/legislation-and-guidelines/`) contains **no SACCO regulations at all**, and the CBK circulars index contains **zero** matches for "sacco" or "co-operative".
2. **The Banking Act's own list of competent authorities names SSRA as a separate body** alongside CBK — not under it (section quoted in Q3 below).
3. **The instrument that does the regulating is the Sacco Societies Act (Cap. 490B)**, whose licensing powers sit with "the Authority" = SSRA.

Consequently there is **no "CBK SACCO Societies (Deposit-Taking) licence"**, no CBK circular setting such capital, and no CBK SACCO fee schedule. CBK's residual role is statutory/representational: the **Governor of the Central Bank (or his representative) sits on the SSRA Board** under s.6(1)(d) of the Sacco Societies Act, and likewise on the Board of Trustees of the Deposit Guarantee Fund.

Also note: **CBK has moved off `cbk.go.ke`.** That domain no longer resolves in DNS (verified 2026-10-20). The live domain is **`centralbank.go.ke`**.

## 1. Minimum core capital

**KES 10,000,000 (ten million shillings)** — absolute floor, *plus* three ratio tests.

**Instrument:** The Sacco Societies (Deposit-Taking Sacco Business) Regulations, **Legal Notice 95 of 2010**, published Kenya Gazette Vol. CXII—No. 62 on 25 June 2010, commenced 25 June 2010; revised by the 24th Annual Supplement (Legal Notice 221 of 2023) as at 31 December 2022.
**URL:** `https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2010/95/eng@2022-12-31` — retrieved 2026-10-20.

Regulation9, verbatim:

> **9. Minimum capital requirements** — A Sacco Society shall at all times maintain—
> (a) core capital of not less than **ten million shillings**;
> (b) core capital of not less than **ten percent of total assets**;
> (c) Institutional capital of not less than **eight percent of total assets**; and
> (d) core capital of not less than **eight percent of total deposits**.

"Core capital" is defined in reg. 3 as fully paid-up members' shares, capital issued, disclosed reserves, retained earnings, grants and donations, none of which are meant to be expended except on liquidation. The ratios are echoed in the capital-adequacy return templates: "Minimum Core Capital to Assets Ratio 10.0%" and "Minimum Core Capital to Deposits requirement 8.0%" (Second Schedule).

SSRA may require **higher** ratios on risk grounds (reg. 10) and may sanction breach by suspending lending/investment, prohibiting land acquisition, or **prohibiting acceptance of further deposits** (reg. 12).

## 2. Maximum deposit ceiling

**No deposit ceiling exists — per-depositor or aggregate — in the deposit-taking regulations or the Sacco Societies Act.** I read both instruments in full for this. There is no provision capping deposits.

What *does* exist is the inverse concept — **deposit protection, not a cap**:

| Figure | Meaning | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **KES 100,000 per member** | Deposit Guarantee Fund compensation ceiling, per member, in respect of all their accounts combined | Sacco Societies Act (Cap. 490B) **s.59(1)**; LN 95/2010 **reg. 80(2)** |
| KES 100,000 net of liabilities | Protected deposit = aggregate credit balance less any liability owed to the society | Sacco Societies Act **s.59(2)** |
| KES 50,000 **or** 0.05% of total savings and deposits (whichever higher) | Annual **Deposit Guarantee Fund premium** paid by each society | LN 95/2010 **reg. 81(1)** |

Note the Deposit Guarantee Fund covers **deposits but not shares** (s.59(1)).

One related restriction worth flagging: LN 95/2010 reg. 15 prohibits a deposit-taking society from "transacting Sacco Society business with non-members" — so deposits are effectively restricted to members, though no monetary cap attaches.

## 3. Credit institution dichotomy — is a deposit-taking SACCO a "credit institution"?

**No — and the term does not appear in the Banking Act at all.** I verified by full-text search: the string "credit institution" occurs **zero times** in the Banking Act (Cap. 488) as at 27 December 2024.

**Instrument:** Banking Act, Cap. 488 (as at 27 December 2024).
**URL:** `https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1989/9/eng@2024-12-27` — retrieved 2026-10-20. (CBK-hosted copy also verified: `https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Banking-Act.pdf` — retrieved 2026-10-20.)

Relevant definitions, s.2, verbatim:

> **"banking business"** means— (a) the accepting from members of the public of money on deposit repayable on demand or at the expiry of a fixed period or after notice; (b) the accepting from members of the public of money on current account and payment on and acceptance of cheques; (c) the employing of money held on deposit or on current account…

> **"financial institution"** means a company other than a bank which carries on…

> **"institution"** means a bank or financial institution or a mortgage finance company;

The closing definition is decisive — a licensed institution is a bank, a financial institution, or a mortgage finance company. A SACCO society, being a co-operative society rather than a company, falls outside it. And the Banking Act's list of **COMPETENT AUTHORITIES** expressly separates the two, with SSRA as its own entry:

> 1. The Central Bank of Kenya; 2. The Capital Markets Authority; 3. The Insurance Regulatory Authority; 4. The Retirement Benefits Authority; 5. The Communications Commission of Kenya; **6. The SACCO Societies Regulatory Authority**; 7. Such other Regulatory Authority established by an Act of Parliament and specified by the Cabinet Secretary…

The Banking Act also refers to SSRA as an established body: where a third-party agency is a co-operative society, "prior approval … shall be sought from the Sacco Societies Regulatory Authority".

**So a SACCO Societies (Deposit-Taking) is categorised under the SACCO SOCIETIES ACT (Cap. 490B), not the Banking Act.** The gating provision, s.23(1), verbatim:

> **23. Carrying out of deposit-taking business** — (1) No person shall carry out deposit-taking business within the meaning of this Act, unless such person— (a) is a Sacco society **registered under the Co-operative Societies Act (Cap. 490)**; and (b) **holds a valid licence issued under this Act.**

Penalties for breach: fine not exceeding KES 500,000, or imprisonment up to three years, or both (s.23(2)).

## 4. Fees

All fees are set by **the Authority (SSRA)**, not CBK, and are in **Legal Notice 95 of 2010** and **Legal Notice 82 of 2020**.

### Deposit-taking SACCO societies — LN 95 of 2010

| Fee | Amount | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Application fee | **KES 3,000** | reg. 4(2)(j) — "(j) an application fee of three thousand shillings" |
| Licence fee — head office | **KES 50,000** | reg. 4(6) |
| Licence fee — **per branch** | **KES 20,000** | reg. 4(6) |
| Annual licence fee | **NOT SPECIFIED** in the regulations | Sacco Societies Act s.25(4) — "such amount and within such time as may be prescribed by the Authority" |
| Deposit Guarantee Fund annual premium | **KES 50,000 or 0.05% of total savings and deposits, whichever is higher** | reg. 81(1) |

Verbatim reg. 4(6): "The Authority shall issue a licence to the applicant Sacco Society upon payment of licence fee of fifty thousand shillings for head office; and twenty thousand shillings for each branch."

Licence runs **to31 December** of the year of issue, renewable on expiry; renewal application due at least **90 days** before expiry (reg. 4(8), reg. 5(1)).

### Non-deposit-taking SACCO societies — LN 82 of 2020

| Authorisation fee — head office | **KES 50,000** | reg. 5(6)(a) |
| Authorisation fee — **per other place of business** | **KES 10,000** | reg. 5(6)(b) |

### Non-deposit-taking category requirements

**Trigger (reg. 4).** Specified non-deposit-taking business is any of: (a) non-deposit-taking business where **total non-withdrawable deposits from members equal or exceed KES 100,000,000**; (b) mobilising membership and share-capital subscription through **digital or electronic payment platforms**; (c) mobilising membership and subscription **from persons ordinarily resident outside the country**. For (b) and (c), written Authority authorisation is required and mobilisation may not be "principally" through those channels without it.

**Capital (reg. 11) — KES 5,000,000, one third of the deposit-taking floor:**
> (a) not less than **five million shillings**; (b) not less than **eight percent of the total assets**, provided at least fifty per cent of core capital be retained earnings and disclosed reserves; and (c) not less than **five percent of total non-withdrawable deposits** held for members.

**Status.** "Authorisation", not licence — revocable, renewable annually, with returns due by **30 November** each year (regs. 5, 9).

**Naming (reg. 7).** Must include in its name **"Regulated Non-withdrawable deposit-taking SACCO"** or **"Regulated Non-WDT-SACCO"**.

**Prohibited business (reg. 16).** May not engage in deposit-taking SACCO business, foreign trade, crypto-currency dealing, trust operations, custodial services, venture capital, securities underwriting/placement, or land purchase (with exceptions).

**Other requirements.** External borrowing capped at **25% of total assets** without written Authority permission (reg. 35). Authorisation may be revoked for falling below **25% of prescribed minimum capital adequacy** (reg. 10(a)). Quarterly capital adequacy, liquidity and non-withdrawable-deposit returns required (regs. 13, 15, 24). Annual publication of authorised societies in the Gazette and a national newspaper (reg. 6).
